Private and confidential

Dear [Employee name],

Confirmation of redundancy

Further to our consultation meetings on [dates], I am writing to confirm that, regrettably, your role of [job title] is redundant and your employment will end by reason of redundancy.

We have explored alternatives to redundancy, including [suitable alternative roles considered], but unfortunately none is available.

Your last day of employment will be [termination date], after your notice period of [notice period]. You will receive:

Statutory redundancy pay of [£ amount]
Pay in lieu of any accrued, untaken holiday
[Any enhanced or contractual payments]

You have the right to appeal this decision. To appeal, please write to [name] within [e.g. 5 working days] setting out your grounds.

I am sorry to be writing in these circumstances. We will support you with [references, time off to seek work, outplacement, etc.]. Please do not hesitate to contact me with any questions.

Yours sincerely,
[Your name]
[Your job title]

What a fair process needs

  • A genuine redundancy situation
  • Fair, objective selection criteria applied consistently
  • Meaningful individual consultation (and collective consultation for 20+)
  • Consideration of suitable alternative employment
  • Correct notice, statutory redundancy pay and a right of appeal

FAQs

Who is entitled to statutory redundancy pay?
Generally employees with at least two years' continuous service, calculated by age, length of service and weekly pay (subject to the statutory cap).
Do you have to consult first?
Yes - meaningful individual consultation is required. Collective consultation rules apply where 20 or more redundancies are proposed within 90 days.
Not legal advice

Redundancy carries significant legal risk, including unfair dismissal claims if the process is not fair. This template is general information, not legal advice - follow a fair process, check current statutory figures, and take professional advice before acting.
