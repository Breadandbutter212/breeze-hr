What to include in a UK offer letter

An offer letter confirms the headline terms and that the role is being offered. To keep it clear and compliant, include:

  • Job title and start date - the role and when it begins.
  • Salary and hours - annual salary (or rate) and weekly hours.
  • Place of work - office, remote or hybrid arrangement.
  • Conditions of the offer - references, right to work, and any checks such as DBS.
  • That a contract will follow - the written statement of particulars must be given on or before the first day of employment.
  • An expiry date - how long the candidate has to accept.

A quick note on compliance

Make any offer conditional on right-to-work and references so it can be withdrawn if those aren't satisfactory. The offer letter doesn't replace the contract of employment - under UK law, employees must receive a written statement of the main terms on or before day one.

Offer letter FAQs

What should a UK offer letter include?
Job title, start date, salary, place of work, working hours, the conditions of the offer (references and right to work), an expiry date, and a note that a full contract of employment will follow. The offer letter doesn't replace the written statement of particulars, which must be provided on or before the first day.
Is an offer letter legally binding in the UK?
It can be, once the candidate accepts - particularly if it sets out the key terms. Most employers make the offer conditional on satisfactory references and proof of right to work, so it can be withdrawn if those conditions aren't met.
What's the difference between an offer letter and a contract?
The offer letter confirms the headline terms and that the role is being offered. The contract of employment is the full legal document covering all terms and conditions, which must be given to the employee on or before day one.
