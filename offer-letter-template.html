What to include in a UK offer letter

  • Job title and start date - the role and when it begins.
  • Salary and hours - annual salary (or rate) and weekly hours.
  • Place of work - office, remote or hybrid arrangement.
  • Conditions of the offer - references, right to work, and any checks such as DBS.
  • That a contract will follow - the written statement of particulars must be given on or before the first day.
  • An expiry date - how long the candidate has to accept.

Offer letter FAQs

What should a UK offer letter include?
Job title, start date, salary, place of work, working hours, the conditions of the offer (references and right to work), an expiry date, and a note that a full contract of employment will follow. It does not replace the written statement of particulars, which must be provided on or before the first day.
Is an offer letter legally binding in the UK?
It can be, once the candidate accepts, particularly if it sets out the key terms. Most employers make the offer conditional on satisfactory references and proof of right to work, so it can be withdrawn if those conditions are not met.
What is the difference between an offer letter and a contract?
The offer letter confirms the headline terms. The contract of employment is the full legal document covering all terms and conditions, which must be given on or before day one.
